# Adoption v2.7 — nominal spending growth

Accepted 21 October 2026. Supersedes v2.6 inverse-price affordability scoring.

Adoption remains 20%: Ramp matched nominal spending growth 10%, OpenRouter
estimated nominal spending growth 10%. Other four pillars and their treatments

## Calculation

For each source, combine raw growth factors before normalization:

spending growth = ((1 + volume growth) × (1 + price change) − 1) × 100,
where the two inputs are fractions. The output is percentage points.
Equivalently, using percentage inputs: g = v + p + v × p / 100.
The page displays volume change, price change, their interaction, and g.
For example, volume +20% and price −10% implies spending +8%.

Each month uses four completed Monday–Sunday weeks ending on the final Sunday.
Changes compare this window with the preceding month's observation window.
Some endpoint intervals are five weeks, leaving a one-week gap; these are not
calendar-month spending or seasonally adjusted growth rates.

Ramp volume and effective price use identical matched model/week pairs within
each window. Price is total matched spending / total matched tokens × 1 million.
All four weeks require at least 95% matched-token coverage. Their growth-factor
product reconciles to the observed matched-spending change. Changing matched
models, business samples, caching and commercial terms can affect that change;
it is not a constant-panel or industry-revenue estimate.

OpenRouter uses its complete 28-day token volume and the arithmetic mean of
Bloomberg SDLLMTK daily saved values over those exact dates. Both windows require
every price date and complete token coverage. This external benchmark has a
different population and model mix, so the resulting spending series is explicitly
a rough proxy, not observed OpenRouter billing or provider revenue. Bloomberg
PREV-filled dates remain carried quotes. The two sources' dollar values are never
added together. Neither normalized component scores nor source totals are multiplied.

## Score and coverage

Each source's percentage spending growth is separately normalized:
score = clip(50 + 15 × (growth − mean) / sample standard deviation, 0, 100).
The fixed calibration months are January–June 2026: six observations per source.
All six are required. Later observations do not enter calibration. The sample is
short, provisional and retrospective, and is not a point-in-time backtest.
Historical source revisions can revise statistics within that accepted window.

December 2025 supplies the first aligned price/volume level; January 2026 supplies
the first growth observation. Earlier v2.7 composites are withheld. Missing current
or prior observations withhold the affected score and composite, with no weight
redistribution. This method is monthly-window growth, rather than YoY growth,
because comparable Bloomberg price history does not yet support a YoY baseline.
